Bike Lifecycle & Maintenance Tracker

Problem

Vehicle running costs were tracked manually in notebooks, making mileage, fuel spend, and service due-dates impossible to analyse. There was no way to see when oil, tyres, or brake pads were due, or what the bike actually cost to own per kilometre.

Solution

Built an automated vehicle-health tracker in Looker Studio connected to Google Sheets and BigQuery. The dashboard calculates odometer and daily averages, counts down parts-replacement intervals, and logs fuel-station history and full ownership cost — all updated from structured trip and expense data.

Impact

Turns scattered logs into a single real-time view of ownership cost and maintenance risk — enabling proactive servicing, clearer cost-per-kilometre analysis, and data-driven decisions that keep the vehicle in optimal condition.

MIS Dashboard Suite for Loan & Deposit Portfolio

Problem

Loan and Deposit portfolio data at Bay Leasing & Investment Ltd. (BLIL) was spread across every branch and pulled manually into static reports. Preparing them was slow and error-prone, and there was no single, interactive view anyone could drill into for timely decisions.

Solution

Solely built a company-wide MIS platform — initiated at the request of the Managing Director — that consolidates Loan and Deposit portfolio data from all branches into one interactive web dashboard, replacing static reports and manual data pulls. Multi-sheet Excel files are parsed in the browser via SheetJS/XLSX (so no database is needed), Chart.js renders KPIs, trend charts, sparklines and portfolio breakdowns, and PHP session-based authentication secures access. It started in November 2025 as a Power BI prototype and was re-architected in April 2026 as a standalone web dashboard for more flexibility, easy deployment on any machine, tighter access control and faster iteration — and remains in active development.

Impact

Senior management uses it for monthly business reviews and meeting presentations, while 15–20 back-office colleagues rely on it for Bangladesh Bank (BB) reporting — cutting 2–3 hours of manual data pulling, sorting and compiling down to a few clicks. Pre-calculated KPIs, time-series data and multi-dimensional filtering (branch, product, RM, customer segment) give instant drill-down, establishing one source of truth across the full company portfolio.

  • Company-wide coverage — every branch's Loan & Deposit portfolio under one interactive interface
  • Saves 15–20 back-office users 2–3 hours per Bangladesh Bank report, now down to a few clicks
  • Pre-calculated KPIs, time-series data and multi-dimensional filtering for instant drill-down
  • Evolved from a Power BI prototype (Nov 2025) to a standalone web app (Apr 2026) — in active development
  • Single source of truth for monthly business reviews, management presentations and regulatory reporting

Global Video Game Sales & Industry Trends

An interactive EDA dashboard built on the Maven Analytics Video Game Sales playground (Kaggle's 2024 VGChartz snapshot). The source lists 64,016 titles released 1971–2024; this build charts the 17,463 platform-title records that carry sales figures — 11,934 distinct titles across 39 consoles and 20 genres, with releases running 1977–2020. It answers the playground's four core questions: which titles sold the most, which year peaked, whether consoles specialize by genre, and which games win one region while flopping in another.

Key Findings

  • GTA V is the best-seller by a wide margin64.29M units across four platforms — about 1% of all tracked units. Its biggest single release is the PS3 version at 20.32M, ahead of every other SKU in the dataset.
  • A long tail, not a handful of hitsThe top 10 titles take just 4.6% of the 6.59B units sold and the top 100 take 19.5% — the market is carried by thousands of mid-sized titles rather than a few blockbusters.
  • Sales peaked in 2008 and the disc era is over2008 releases alone moved 537.84M units; the 2000s produced 3.32B vs 2.52B in the 2010s (down 24%). Part of the fall is an artefact — these are lifetime physical units, so recent years haven't finished accumulating.
  • North America is half the global marketNA 50.7%, Europe/PAL 29.0%, Japan 10.3% and the rest of the world 9.9% of 6.59B units.
  • Consoles really do specialize by genreWii U leans Music (11% of its units vs 0.8% market-wide, 13.9×) and Action-Adventure (8.9×); Game Boy leans Puzzle (7.1×); DS leans Simulation (19%, 4.3×); X360, PS4 and Xbox One lean Shooter (27–34%).
  • Regional hits and flops are starkHot Shots Golf sold 76% of its units in Japan (2.13M) against 0.29M in North America; FIFA 15 took 69% in Europe (12.45M) but only 0.15M in Japan; GTA V did 26.19M in NA and just 1.66M in Japan. Xbox One drew only 0.1% of its units from Japan, the PSP 21.1%.
  • Critic scores barely move unitsOnly 22.8% of records carry a score (3,984 of 17,463) and the correlation with sales is moderate at best (r = 0.29). The 50 best-sellers average 8.84/10 vs 7.11/10 overall — reviews help at the top, but brand and IP do the selling.
  • Publishing power is concentratedActivision leads with 11.0% of all units, then Electronic Arts (9.8%) and EA Sports (7.4%) — the top three control 28.1% across 672 publishers, with a very long tail behind them.
Method note: units are lifetime physical copies in millions (VGChartz 2024 snapshot) — digital and mobile sales aren't counted, which is why 2019 and 2020 look almost empty (2.55M and 3.45M). Averages and correlations use only the 3,984 records that have a critic score. A further 5,955 titles are listed in the source without sales figures and are excluded from every total.

AI Usage & Impact on Students and Professionals

A single-file, fully interactive EDA dashboard built on the public Kaggle dataset "AI Usage and Impact on Students & Professionals" — 288 respondents across 12 countries, split almost evenly between students (52%) and working professionals (48%). It explores who uses AI, which tools and purposes dominate, and whether usage actually moves productivity, satisfaction and time saved. Every chart is click-to-filter, and every figure below comes straight from the dataset.

Key Findings

  • ChatGPT is the default — 41% of respondents119 of 288 name it as their primary AI tool; ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Claude together cover ~77% of the sample.
  • Coding is the #1 use case — but Writing saves the most timeCoding leads at 22.6%, then Writing (17%), Studying (13.5%) and Research (13.2%); Writing users report ~7 hrs/week saved vs ~5.6 for Coding.
  • Students are not heavier users than professionals3.01 vs 2.87 hrs/day — effectively identical, even though professionals work longer days (8.3 vs 6.2 hrs).
  • Impact is real but modest — accuracy is the weak linkAverage productivity 5.24/10, satisfaction 6.13/10 and 5.81 hrs saved per week — but accuracy scores only 3.42/10.
  • More usage → more time saved and perceived productivity, not accuracyHours/day correlates 0.78 with weekly time saved and 0.66 with productivity, but only 0.05 with accuracy.
  • 59% would recommend AI; 1 in 4 would not170 Yes, 71 No, 42 Maybe; students slightly more positive (60.3%) than professionals (57.7%).
  • Young, global, degree-heavy sampleMedian age 27, 44% aged 16–25, 66% hold a Bachelor's or Master's; India, US, Canada, Pakistan and the UK lead respondent counts.
  • Tool choice barely moves productivityCopilot 5.51, Claude 5.29 and ChatGPT 5.23 cluster together while Perplexity trails at 4.64 — differences sit within noise, so usage intensity matters more than the brand.
Method note: averages use non-null values only (14 records lack a productivity score, 7 satisfaction, 10 time-saved, 11 income); percentages are over all 288 respondents.

Interactive Web Game

Flying Tesla of BD — Mission Impossible

Elon Musk spent a decade and a few billion dollars trying to put a Tesla in the sky. I spent a few weekends putting a rickshaw in one instead — bolted-on thrusters, a Dhaka skyline absolutely covered in live wires, and approximately 0% useful outcome. It is a browser game, it is free, and it is very serious about not being serious.

No engine, no framework, no image files, no audio files. Everything you see is drawn in code and everything you hear is synthesized on the spot, mostly because downloading an asset felt like too much effort.

  • Every pixel drawn in code — rickshaw, wires, leaning poles, parallax skyline, all of it
  • All sound synthesized live with WebAudio, including the crash you will hear a lot
  • Wires generated on the fly, always leaving a gap wide enough for you to miss and blame yourself
  • Load-shedding mode: screen dims, rickshaw autopilots for 5 poles, you tap uselessly
  • Pause, mute, and a high score your browser remembers long after you close the tab in shame
